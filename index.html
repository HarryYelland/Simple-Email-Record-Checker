Start here

Anyone can write any "From" address on an email. Nothing checks it by default. So someone can send an email that says it's from you@yourcompany.com when it isn't. This is called spoofing. Scammers use it to trick your customers and staff.

SPF, DKIM and DMARC are three settings you add to your domain. They let Gmail, Outlook and other email services check whether an email really came from you.

SPF

A list of the services that can send email as your domain, such as Google Workspace or Mailchimp. Email from anywhere else looks suspicious.

DKIM

A digital signature added to each email you send. The receiving service checks it. It shows the email came from you and wasn't changed on the way.

DMARC

Says what to do with email that fails the SPF and DKIM checks: do nothing, put it in spam, or block it. It also sends you reports on who is sending email as you.

You need all three. SPF can fail when an email is forwarded, but DKIM usually still works. DMARC makes sure the checks are for your domain and tells receivers what to do when they fail. Google and Yahoo require all three from anyone who sends a lot of email (about 5,000 a day or more).

Nothing matches that. Try a shorter word, like spf, key or report.

Moving from watching to blocking

      
    
        
  1. Start with "Just watch". Nothing can break.
    2. 
        
  2. Read the reports for two to four weeks. Look for legitimate services (newsletters, invoicing, helpdesk) that aren't passing, and ask them how to set up SPF or DKIM for your domain.
    3. 
        
  3. Switch to "Send fakes to spam". Optionally start at 10% in More options and raise it over time.
    4. 
        
  4. Finish with "Block fakes completely".
    5. 
      

      
The reports arrive as technical files. A free DMARC report reader service can turn them into a readable weekly summary.

    

  


  
  

    

      
Set up DKIM the email signature

      
DKIM adds a digital signature to each email you send. Receivers use it to check the email is real and wasn't changed on the way.

      
You usually don't need to create anything yourself. Your email provider makes the key. You switch it on and copy the record it gives you into your domain. Pick your provider:

      
Google Workspace (Gmail for business)
        
    
          
  1. Sign in to the Google Admin console (admin.google.com) as an administrator.
    2. 
          
  2. Go to Apps > Google Workspace > Gmail > Authenticate email.
    3. 
          
  3. Choose your domain and click Generate new record (2048-bit is best).
    4. 
          
  4. Copy the Name and Value it shows into your domain's DNS as a TXT record (see "How do I add this?" below).
    5. 
          
  5. Return to the Admin console and click Start authentication. This can take up to 48 hours to show as active.
    6. 
        

      

      
Microsoft 365 (Outlook for business)
        
    
          
  1. Sign in to the Microsoft Defender portal (security.microsoft.com) as an administrator.
    2. 
          
  2. Go to Email & collaboration > Policies & rules > Threat policies > Email authentication settings, then the DKIM tab.
    3. 
          
  3. Select your domain. Microsoft shows two CNAME records (names starting selector1._domainkey and selector2._domainkey).
    4. 
          
  4. Add both to your domain's DNS exactly as shown. They are CNAME records, not TXT.
    5. 
          
  5. Back in the portal, switch Sign messages for this domain with DKIM signatures to on.
    6. 
        

      

      
Mailchimp, SendGrid, Mailgun, or another service
        
Open that service's settings and look for Domain authentication, Sender authentication or DKIM. It will show you records to copy into your domain's DNS. Follow its on-screen steps, then use "Check my domain" to confirm.
